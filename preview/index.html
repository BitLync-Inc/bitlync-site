How Bitlync works

One model. The MSP connects their tools.

One model underneath. Your product uses one API. The MSP connects their own tools.

01 — Your product integrates once

One schema, every vendor

Write against one model: tickets, companies, contacts, and agreements, using Bitlync words. Devices too, where that connector exists. No per-vendor branches in your codebase.

02 — Customers connect themselves

Self-serve, guided, validated

The MSP pastes their own PSA or RMM keys. You never hold those credentials. ISVs start with docs and a sandbox, not a wiring guide per MSP.

03 — Act through the stack

Read and write, safely

Open a ticket or update a device through the API. Writes that already support it take dry_run and an idempotency_key. Creating a leftover PSA company in the ISV is not shipped yet.

Everything that comes with an integration

Every layer you'd otherwise build, bundled

These are not add-ons. Caching, retries, validation, and the vault ship with every connection.

Caching

Reads come from our synced store. They are fast and do not spend the vendor rate budget. Each response says where it came from and when it was synced.

Rate-limit handling

We pace every call against each vendor's real limits so your product never trips them by accident.

Retries

Transient vendor failures are retried with backoff. You see one clean result, not a flaky upstream.

Honest delivery status

Deduped and retried. No silent drop. An acknowledged write cannot double-write. A throttled one returns a real Retry-After.

De-duplication

Idempotency keys on writes that support them mean a retry does not double-create a ticket.

Validation

Payloads are checked against each vendor’s rules before they leave. Errors show up in your request, not in an MSP’s PSA.

Credential vault

The MSP pastes their own PSA or RMM keys. Those credentials sit in a per-tenant vault. Your product never holds them.

Customer map

A sure match maps automatically. A leftover PSA company is an ask to create in the ISV. Ambiguous stays unmatched. No silent create. The create hop itself is not shipped yet.

Identity hold

Unmatched records can be held in quarantine until someone releases them.

Health + incidents

Connection status and an audit log are in the product. The vendor-health board on this page is a mockup.

Grants, not shared credentials

You own the connection. Vendors get grants.

You connect your PSA or RMM to Bitlync once. That connection is yours. Each vendor gets a separate, scoped grant. You enter the credential on our side. It stays in an isolated per-tenant vault. The vendor never sees it. Revoke a grant and that vendor is gone. Your connection stays yours.

What a vendor can touch

You see the scope before you connect

Every grant is shown in plain language, including what the vendor can never do:

Readservice tickets, companies, configurations
Writecreate tickets on a board you approve
Neverdelete records · read financials · change your configuration

Example grant — every vendor's scope is shown at connect time

Our data posture

Plainly stated

Synced once per tenant

One governed sync serves every vendor you've granted. Five tools never mean five pollers spending your shared API rate budget.

Encrypted per-tenant

AES-256-GCM envelope encryption with per-tenant keys. Your tenant is isolated from every other.

Owned by you

Every grant is revocable in one click. Your data is deletable on request.

Audited, append-only

Every read and write by every vendor lands in an append-only log. It cannot be edited. You can see it.

Support that can't touch your data

The support assistant is read-only. It has no write tool. It can diagnose your setup. It cannot change it.

Never resold

Your operational data is not a product. Not aggregated, not sold. Ever.

Quickstart

From key to first write.

Seeded tenants are fixture-backed only. Use $BITLYNC_BASE as the host placeholder.

Keys look like blk_…. Send them as X-API-Key.

auth
X-API-Key: blk_…

tenant_id: sandbox-mainstreet
# or sandbox-northline

sandbox-mainstreet and sandbox-northline are sandbox fixtures (PSA and RMM seed data). They are not production tenants.

GET tickets

GET tickets
curl -s "$BITLYNC_BASE/tenants/sandbox-mainstreet/tickets" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $BITLYNC_KEY"

POST tickets

dry_run defaults true. Commit by sending dry_run: false plus an idempotency_key. The same key on a retry returns idempotent_replay.

POST /tenants/{tenant_id}/tickets

Sandbox reset

POST /sandbox/reset with {"tenant_id":"sandbox-mainstreet"}. Clears audit, re-syncs fixtures, writes one sandbox_reset event. Production tenants return 422 request_invalid.

POST /sandbox/reset
curl -s -X POST "$BITLYNC_BASE/sandbox/reset" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $BITLYNC_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"tenant_id":"sandbox-mainstreet"}'

Use cases

What you can build.

Six paths. Honest labels. Device inventory is not a use case.

1. Alert to ticket live

A finding or alert becomes a ticket. Incident and approval sit on this same path. Do not split them.

2. Map customers live

Sure match auto-maps. A leftover PSA company is an ask. Ambiguous stays unmatched. The create-into-ISV hop is Coming.

3. Billing reconciliation Coming

Agreement-shaped billing later. There is no GET /agreements today.

4. Agent deploy

POST /tenants/{tenant_id}/deployments.

5. Time on the ticket later

Time entries exist in schema. Mapping is still next-slice. No route.

6. Close with evidence later

Close a ticket with the work attached. Not shipped.
